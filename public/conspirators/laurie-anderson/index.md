# Laurie Anderson

[Laurie Anderson](https://laurieanderson.com/) is an American artist, composer and musician, born in Chicago in 1947. She trained in violin and sculpture, made performance work in New York through the 1970s, and reached number two on the UK singles chart in 1981 with [“O Superman”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Superman). She builds her own instruments, among them the tape-bow violin of 1977, which replaces the horsehair of the bow with recorded magnetic tape and puts a tape head in the bridge.

She met [Lou Reed](/conspirators/lou-reed/) in 1992 and was married to him from April 2008 until his death in 2013. Sarth Calhoun played with the two of them on the [Yellow Pony](/conspiracies/yellow-pony/) tours in 2009.

Yellow Pony, Madrid, July 2009.

## Yellow Pony

The tours were billed *The Yellow Pony and Other Songs and Stories*: two short European legs in 2009, one in July and one across August and September, eight cities from Sant Feliu de Guíxols to Paris. Anderson stood with violin and electronics, Reed sat with guitars, and Sarth was between them.

From the Yellow Pony page of Sarth’s own site: In 2009 I had the unique opportunity to do two short tours with Lou Reed and Laurie Anderson. I played continuum fingerboard and did live resampling of their voices. Also, I got to stand in the center of the stage, like a drummer. It was a wonderful experience, and it is also when I fell in love with Spain.

## What the room looked like

Dave Simpson reviewed the Manchester night for [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2009/jul/20/laurie-anderson-lou-reed-review) on 20 July 2009: The two titans faced each other – Anderson (standing) on violin and electronics, and Reed (seated) on various guitars – with electronic sound manipulator Sarth Calhoun between them. Over two hours, his expression transformed from enthusiastic glee to that of a baffled marriage counsellor, as Reed barked instructions.

The Paris house programme at Salle Pleyel billed him as “claviers et électronique.” The full run is on [Appearances](/appearances/).

## Vivid LIVE

Anderson and Reed curated the 2010 edition of Vivid LIVE at the [Sydney Opera House](https://www.sydneyoperahouse.com/vivid-live/past-festival-lineups), running 27 May to 11 June. Their programme included Anderson’s own *Transitory Life*, a Noise Night, a tai chi event called *Power and Serenity*, and [Lou Reed’s Metal Machine Trio](/conspiracies/metal-machine-trio/), which played the Opera Theatre on 30 May with Sarth on Continuum and laptops.

## Afterwards

Sarth and Anderson have played together at several events built around Reed’s work since he died. In March 2019 they performed at the Cathedral of St. John the Divine for what would have been Reed’s seventy-seventh birthday, with [John Zorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Zorn) on organ, Stewart Hurwood running Reed’s guitars and amplifiers, Stan Harrison and Shahzad Ismaily, and tai chi led by Master Ren Guang-Yi.

That August, at the first International Lou Reed Tai Chi Day, they played the Prospect Park Bandshell for BRIC Celebrate Brooklyn!, a Master Ren demonstration accompanied by Reed’s Drones. [Brooklyn Vegan](https://www.brooklynvegan.com/lou-reed-tai-chi-day-is-aug-3-laurie-anderson-john-zorn-celebrating-in-brooklyn/) announced the lineup as a live performance of Reed’s “Drones” featuring Laurie Anderson, John Zorn, Sarth Calhoun and Stewart Hurwood.

They played again on 2 March 2023 at Brookfield Place, for Reed’s eighty-first birthday, with [Kevin Hearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Hearn) and Shahzad Ismaily, against Hurwood’s drones. Reed had left an unfinished book about tai chi. Anderson decided to complete it and worked on it with three of his friends, Stephan Berwick, Robert Currie and Scott Richman; *The Art of the Straight Line: My Tai Chi* came out that spring. Sarth is one of the people they interviewed for it, as [The Kitchen Sisters](https://kitchensisters.org/podcast/lou-reeds-tai-chi/) record.
